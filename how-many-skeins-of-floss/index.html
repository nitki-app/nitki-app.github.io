How Many Skeins of DMC Floss Do I Need?

A standard DMC stranded cotton skein is 8 m (8.7 yd) of six strands. How far it goes depends on the fabric count and how many strands you stitch with. Here is a simple way to count skeins for any pattern, and tables you can use straight away.

The quick rule

With two strands on 14-count Aida, one skein covers roughly 1,500–1,800 full cross stitches. On finer fabric each stitch is smaller and uses less thread, so the same skein goes further. For each color in your pattern, divide its number of stitches by the coverage and round up. Then add one spare skein of the colors you use most: dye lots differ, and running out of one color halfway through is the worst way to find out.

Counted at about 1,600 stitches per skein on 14 count, 1,800 on 16 count and 2,000 on 18 count. Stitching with one strand roughly doubles the coverage; three strands cut it by a third.

A whole project

Most of the floss goes to a few big colors, but every color needs at least one skein. That is why the number of colors matters as much as the size. Two real examples:

A small 60 × 75 ornament in 15 colors usually needs about 15 skeins, one of each color, with plenty left over. If you are making several ornaments, choose the same palette for all of them and the leftovers become the next ornament.

Other things that use floss

  • Half stitches use about half the thread of a full cross.
  • Backstitch outlines are usually one strand and use very little, but they need their own skein if the color isn't used elsewhere.
  • Long jumps across the back between areas of one color waste thread. Finish an area, then start the next one fresh.
